---
name: Mobile recording camera ownership
description: Why iOS recording and live WebRTC must share one native camera session
---

On iOS, local recording and preview must use one shared native capture session whenever that camera module exists. Do not make native recording selection depend on the optional WebRTC bridge being detectable at JS module import time. Live video may degrade independently without switching recording back to CameraView. Start Live before native movie recording; do not initialize WebRTC audio or present Live controls after movie capture has begun. Keep the auxiliary WebRTC frame output in encoder-native NV12 and prove at least one frame reached its source before advertising video.

**Why:** Physical iPad testing showed controls freezing after recording began and viewers failing to connect. A static WebRTC capability gate disabled the native recorder too, allowing CameraView to finalize after 35 seconds while JS still showed REC and stamped later events. Live signaling also raced its initial score-only join against a later video-ready join. A later physical test activated Live after recording began; the app rotated and the uploaded master finalized at roughly 14 seconds while the game continued for about a minute.

**How to apply:** Select native recording from native-camera availability alone. Discover the WebRTC bridge lazily, advertise shared video only after stream creation plus first-frame acknowledgement, and serialize video-mode upgrades after the server's broadcaster acknowledgement. Prepare, share, and activate Live before starting recording; once recording is active, Live controls may display status but must not start or present native UI. Any recording promise that resolves must retain its URI and end stale REC/timeline state. Require physical cold-entry, simultaneous Live + recording, background/foreground, and sustained recording tests for every shared-session binary.
