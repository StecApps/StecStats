Here is the checklist that got PayDock through Apple review, plus the important lessons from the rejections.

1. Apple Developer account and agreements
Before submitting:

Apple Developer Program membership is active.
Paid Applications Agreement is accepted.
Banking and tax information is complete in App Store Connect.
The app’s bundle ID matches the uploaded build.
The app is listed as Free if downloads are intended to be free.
The paid feature is configured as an auto-renewable in-app subscription, not as the app’s purchase price.
For PayDock:

App download: Free
Subscription: $4.99/month
Trial: two weeks
Subscription type: Apple In-App Purchase
2. Login and App Review access
If the app requires login, Apple needs to be able to enter the app immediately.

Provide in App Review Information:

A dedicated reviewer email address
The password
Step-by-step login instructions
Any special navigation instructions
Confirmation that the account is already populated with sample data
The demo account should contain realistic content so the reviewer can see the actual product.

For PayDock, the review account had:

Sample bills
Different due dates and categories
A monthly budget
Sample saved login entries
Access to the Account and Subscription screens
Do not make the reviewer:

Register a new account
Wait for an invitation
Verify an email
Provide a phone number
Contact you for access
Complete an unavailable external setup step
If the app offers Google, Facebook, or another third-party login, Apple may require Sign in with Apple. PayDock avoided that issue by using email/password sign-in only.

3. Account deletion
If users can create an account, provide a working way to delete it.

For PayDock, this included:

An in-app Delete account control
A clear confirmation step
Permanent deletion of the user’s associated data
A public deletion-information URL: https://paydock.app/delete-account
The deletion flow should explain what is removed and what happens to subscriptions. App Store subscriptions generally need to be canceled separately through Apple’s subscription management.

4. In-app purchases and subscriptions
For digital features, use Apple’s In-App Purchase system.

The app must not send iOS users to:

Stripe Checkout
A web payment page
PayPal
Another external payment provider
The subscription screen should clearly show:

Product name
Price
Billing period
Trial terms, if applicable
What the subscription unlocks
Automatic-renewal information
A purchase button
Restore Purchases
Subscription management/cancellation guidance
The issue that caused PayDock’s rejection
Having a hosted Terms page was not enough. Apple required the Terms of Use/EULA link to be visible directly inside the subscription purchase section.

The corrected PayDock flow showed both of these links beside the subscription details:

Terms of Use / EULA
Privacy Policy
Both links had to open successfully.

For PayDock:

EULA: https://paydock.app/terms
Privacy Policy: https://paydock.app/privacy
The subscription group, subscription product, and app version also needed to be submitted together for review.

5. Restore and subscription management
Include and test:

Restore Purchases
Existing-purchase recognition after reinstall
Subscription entitlement activation
Subscription status after returning to the app
A Manage Subscription option that opens Apple’s subscription management page
Apple reviewers should be able to understand how a user:

Purchases Pro
Restores Pro
Cancels Pro
Manages renewal
6. Privacy, legal, and support information
The following must be present in App Store Connect and working online:

Privacy Policy URL
Terms of Use/EULA URL
Support URL
Marketing URL, if used
Accurate app description
Accurate subscription disclosure
The privacy policy should describe:

Account information
Bills and financial-related data
Stored credentials
Notifications
Household sharing
Data storage
Third-party services
Account deletion
Data retention
The URL should be public, HTTPS, and return a successful response without requiring login.

7. App Store metadata
Complete:

App name
Subtitle
Description
Keywords
Primary category
Age rating
Copyright
Privacy disclosures
App icon
Screenshots
App preview, if used
“What’s New” text for updates
Screenshots should:

Show the real app
Match the submitted version
Use Apple-supported device dimensions
Avoid placeholder or misleading content
Demonstrate the primary features
For a login-based app, include screenshots that show enough of the product for the reviewer to understand its purpose.

8. App Review notes
The review notes should explain exactly how to test the app.

A strong format is:

Sign in using the demo credentials.
Open the main feature.
Open Account or Settings.
Navigate to the subscription section.
Confirm the price and trial.
Confirm the EULA and Privacy Policy links.
Test or cancel the purchase in Apple’s sandbox.
Tap Restore Purchases.
Explain how to manage or cancel the subscription.
Also tell Apple:

Whether internet access is required
Whether push notifications are optional
Whether the app supports iPhone only or iPad
Which storefront/country the app targets
Whether purchases use StoreKit
What the free tier includes
What the paid tier unlocks
9. Technical testing before submission
Test the exact release build through TestFlight on a real iPhone:

Fresh install
Login with the reviewer account
Main navigation
All primary actions
Subscription screen
Purchase flow
Restore Purchases
EULA link
Privacy link
Account deletion
Logout and relogin
Push notification permission handling
App relaunch
Network error states
No crashes or blank screens
Also verify:

The version number and build number are correct.
There are no development URLs or placeholder keys.
The app icon matches the intended branding.
All external links use HTTPS.
The app’s actual behavior matches the App Store description.
Encryption/export-compliance questions are completed.
The short version
The most important requirements were:

Give Apple working demo credentials.
Preload the reviewer account with usable demo data.
Keep the app download free if the product is subscription-based.
Use Apple IAP for digital subscriptions.
Submit the subscription product with the app version.
Show the subscription price and terms clearly.
Put visible EULA and Privacy Policy links directly in the purchase section.
Make Restore Purchases work.
Provide in-app account deletion.
Keep the privacy, terms, support, screenshots, and review instructions accurate and live.
For another app, the safest approach is to complete all of those items before submitting the first build, especially the reviewer account and the in-app EULA/Privacy links.
